01
To study CE amplifier with variation of load resistance.
CO1 ⚡ Falstad Potentiometer slider + AC input generator
Simulate ↗
💡 Core Theory & Governing Equations
The Common Emitter (CE) configuration provides both current and voltage amplification with a 180° phase inversion between the base input and collector output. The voltage gain is determined by the effective AC collector load:
A_v = - (R_C || R_L) / r'_e  |  r'_e ≈ 26 mV / I_E
Decreasing the load resistance R_L reduces the parallel combination (R_C || R_L), resulting in reduced AC voltage gain but wider bandwidth.
❓ Essential Viva-Voce Questions
Q1: Why is there a 180° phase difference between input and output in a CE amplifier?
As the input base voltage rises, base current increases, driving a higher collector current I_C. The higher voltage drop across R_C causes the collector output voltage V_C = V_CC - I_C*R_C to drop, creating a 180° phase inversion.
Q2: What is the role of the emitter bypass capacitor C_E?
It provides a low-impedance bypass path to ground for AC signals, preventing AC negative feedback across R_E and maximizing the AC voltage gain while preserving DC bias stability.
Q3: Why is the CE configuration most widely used in multi-stage audio amplifiers?
Because it is the only BJT configuration that delivers both high voltage gain (A_v) and high current gain (A_i), producing the highest overall power gain (A_p = A_v × A_i).
⚠️
Lab Pro-Tip: Keep the AC generator input voltage below 20 mV_p-p to prevent non-linear clipping distortion at the collector output.

To study and verify the operation of inverting mode of op-amp using IC 741.
CO1 ⚡ Falstad Interactive AC source + Feedback resistor
Simulate ↗
💡 Core Theory & Governing Equations
The input signal is applied to the inverting terminal (Pin 2) via R1, while the non-inverting terminal (Pin 3) is grounded. Negative feedback is applied through Rf:
V_out = - (R_f / R_1) × V_in  |  Closed-Loop Gain A_CL = - R_f / R_1
Because the non-inverting input is grounded and open-loop gain A_OL → ∞, the inverting terminal is maintained at a "Virtual Ground" (0 V).
❓ Essential Viva-Voce Questions
Q1: What is the virtual ground concept and why does it occur?
Since open-loop gain A_OL is extremely large (~200,000) and output voltage is finite (clamped by ±V_CC), the differential input voltage V_d = V(+) - V(-) = V_out / A_OL ≈ 0. Since Pin 3 is tied to physical ground (0 V), Pin 2 is held at 0 V (virtual ground) without direct ground connection.
Q2: What is Slew Rate in an operational amplifier?
Slew Rate is the maximum rate of change of output voltage per unit time, specified in V/µs. For IC 741, typical slew rate is 0.5 V/µs, primarily limited by the internal 30 pF compensation capacitor.
Q3: What are the pin connections for power supply and offset null in IC 741?
Pin 7 is +V_CC (+12V to +15V), Pin 4 is -V_EE (-12V to -15V), Pin 2 is inverting input, Pin 3 is non-inverting input, Pin 6 is output, and Pins 1 & 5 are for offset null potentiometer trimming.
⚠️
Lab Pro-Tip: Always connect both positive (+V_CC) and negative (-V_EE) DC power supplies with common reference to ground before connecting the signal generator.

To study and verify the operation of non-inverting mode of op-amp using IC 741.
CO1 ⚡ Falstad Interactive AC source + Resistor network
Simulate ↗
💡 Core Theory & Governing Equations
The input signal is applied directly to the non-inverting terminal (Pin 3). A fraction of the output is fed back to the inverting input (Pin 2) via the voltage divider R_f and R_1:
V_out = (1 + R_f / R_1) × V_in  |  Closed-Loop Gain A_CL = 1 + R_f / R_1 ≥ 1
The output voltage is in-phase with the input (0° phase shift) and features extremely high input impedance (hundreds of MΩ).
❓ Essential Viva-Voce Questions
Q1: What is a Voltage Follower (Unity Gain Buffer)?
When R_f = 0 Ω (direct short) and R_1 = ∞ (open), gain becomes A_v = 1. It acts as a voltage buffer that isolates stages: it has very high input impedance (does not load prior stage) and near-zero output impedance.
Q2: Can the gain of a non-inverting op-amp be less than 1?
No. Since A_CL = 1 + (R_f / R_1) and physical resistors have positive resistance values, the minimum theoretical gain is exactly 1 (unity).
Q3: Why is the input impedance of non-inverting mode much higher than inverting mode?
In non-inverting mode, input enters directly into the gate/base of the op-amp input differential pair, where negative feedback boosts input resistance by (1 + A_OL*β). In inverting mode, input sees resistor R1 directly to virtual ground.
⚠️
Lab Pro-Tip: Remember the Gain-Bandwidth Product (GBW = 1 MHz for 741). If you design for a gain of 100, the maximum usable bandwidth drops to 10 kHz.

💡 Core Theory & Truth Tables
Logic gates form the bedrock of digital computing. TTL 74xx IC numbers:
7408 (AND) | 7432 (OR) | 7404 (NOT) | 7400 (NAND) | 7402 (NOR) | 7486 (XOR)
Standard TTL operates at V_CC = +5.0V ± 5%, where HIGH is 2.0V–5.0V and LOW is 0.0V–0.8V.
❓ Essential Viva-Voce Questions
Q1: Why are NAND and NOR known as universal gates?
Because any basic gate (AND, OR, NOT) and any combinational logic expression can be synthesized exclusively using only NAND gates or only NOR gates.
Q2: What is Fan-Out and Propagation Delay in digital logic?
Fan-Out is the maximum number of standard logic inputs a gate output can reliably drive without voltage degradation (typical TTL Fan-Out is 10). Propagation Delay is the time elapsed between input transition and output response (~10 ns in standard TTL).
Q3: What is the Boolean expression for a 2-input XOR gate?
Y = A ⊕ B = A'·B + A·B'. The output is HIGH (1) if and only if the inputs are different (odd parity detector).
⚠️
Lab Pro-Tip: In physical TTL circuits, never leave unused inputs floating—connect them to V_CC via a 1 kΩ pull-up resistor to prevent noise triggering.

To realize Half Adder and Half Subtractor using logic gates.
CO2 ⚡ Falstad Inputs: Logic switches for Sum & Carry
Simulate ↗
💡 Core Theory & Logic Expressions
A Half Adder computes the arithmetic sum of two 1-bit numbers. A Half Subtractor computes the difference between two 1-bit numbers:
Half Adder: Sum = A ⊕ B , Carry = A · B
Half Subtractor: Difference = A ⊕ B , Borrow = A' · B
Notice that the Sum and Difference outputs share the identical XOR logic function.
❓ Essential Viva-Voce Questions
Q1: How many two-input NAND gates are needed to construct a Half Adder?
Exactly 5 NAND gates (4 NAND gates to produce Sum = A ⊕ B, and inverting the output of the first cross-coupled NAND gate gives Carry = A · B).
Q2: What is the fundamental limitation of a Half Adder in arithmetic ALUs?
A Half Adder accepts only two inputs (A and B) and has no provision to receive an incoming carry (C_in) generated from an earlier lower-order bit addition. Multi-bit addition requires Full Adders.
Q3: When is the Borrow output HIGH in a Half Subtractor?
Only when A = 0 and B = 1 (computing 0 - 1 = 1 with a borrow of 1, effectively borrowing from the next higher 2^1 radix position).
⚠️
Lab Pro-Tip: Verify that when inputs are A=1, B=1, the Half Adder output is Sum=0 and Carry=1 (binary 10_2 = 2_10).

To verify the operation of multiplexer using IC 74153 (Dual 4:1 MUX).
CO3 🌐 CircuitVerse Select lines (S0, S1) + Data lines (I0-I3)
CircuitVerse ↗
💡 Core Theory & Logic Equations
IC 74153 is a Dual 4-line to 1-line multiplexer. It contains two separate 4:1 multiplexers sharing address select lines S1 (Pin 2) and S0 (Pin 14), each with an independent active-low enable strobe (1G' on Pin 1, 2G' on Pin 15):
Y = S1'·S0'·I0 + S1'·S0·I1 + S1·S0'·I2 + S1·S0·I3  (when Strobe G' = 0)
When Strobe G' = 1, output Y is clamped to logic 0.
❓ Essential Viva-Voce Questions
Q1: What is the function of the Strobe (Enable) pin G' in IC 74153?
It is an active-low control line. When G' = HIGH (1), the multiplexer is disabled and output Y is forced LOW regardless of inputs. When G' = LOW (0), normal data selection occurs.
Q2: How can a 4:1 multiplexer be used to implement any 3-variable logic function?
Assign two variables to select lines S1 and S0. The four data inputs (I0–I3) are connected to 0, 1, the third variable C, or its complement C' according to the truth table without needing external gates.
Q3: How many select lines are needed for an N-to-1 multiplexer?
n = log_2(N) select lines. For instance, 4:1 needs 2 select lines, 8:1 needs 3 select lines, and 16:1 needs 4 select lines.
⚠️
Lab Pro-Tip: Don't forget to tie Strobe (Pin 1 or 15) to GND (0 V) during testing, otherwise output remains stuck at 0!

💡 Core Theory & Logic Equations
A Demultiplexer takes a single input data line and routes it to one of 2^n output lines based on n select lines. IC 74155 is a Dual 1:4 Demultiplexer / 2:4 Decoder with active-low outputs:
Outputs Y0', Y1', Y2', Y3' are active LOW (0 when selected)
Section 1 has an active-low data input (1C), while Section 2 has an active-high data input (2C').
❓ Essential Viva-Voce Questions
Q1: What is the relationship between a Demultiplexer and a Decoder?
A demultiplexer functions identically to a decoder if its data input line is permanently held at active logic level. The select lines then act as decoder address inputs.
Q2: Why does IC 74155 feature active-low outputs?
TTL logic offers greater sink current (I_OL) than source current (I_OH), allowing active-low outputs to drive external logic or indicators with superior noise immunity.
Q3: How can IC 74155 be expanded to act as a 1:8 Demultiplexer or 3-to-8 line decoder?
Connect select lines S0 and S1 together, and use the 3rd select line to drive the complementary data inputs 1C and 2C' simultaneously.
⚠️
Lab Pro-Tip: Since outputs are active-LOW, connect indicator LEDs to +5V through 330Ω resistors so they illuminate when active.

To verify the operation of 4-bit comparator.
CO3 🌐 CircuitVerse CircuitVerse: 4-bit Magnitude Comparator
CircuitVerse ↗
💡 Core Theory & Logic Equations
A 4-bit magnitude comparator compares two binary words A (A3..A0) and B (B3..B0). It asserts one of three mutually exclusive outputs:
A > B  |  A = B  |  A < B
Equality occurs when all bit pairs match: (A3 ⊙ B3)(A2 ⊙ B2)(A1 ⊙ B1)(A0 ⊙ B0) = 1 (using XNOR gates).
❓ Essential Viva-Voce Questions
Q1: Which bit position takes precedence during magnitude comparison?
The Most Significant Bit (MSB, A3 and B3). If A3 ≠ B3, the output is determined immediately, ignoring all lower-order bits (A2..A0).
Q2: What are cascading inputs in a comparator like IC 7485?
Inputs (I_A>B, I_A=B, I_A<B) allow chaining multiple 4-bit comparator ICs in cascade to compare 8-bit, 12-bit, or larger words without extra logic gates.
Q3: For a standalone 4-bit comparison, how must cascading inputs be connected?
The I_A=B input must be tied to V_CC (+5V), while I_A>B and I_A<B must be tied to ground (0V).
⚠️
Lab Pro-Tip: Always test corner cases: all 0s vs all 0s (A=B), 1000 vs 0111 (A>B), and 1110 vs 1111 (A<B).

💡 Core Theory & Governing Equations
A Junction Field-Effect Transistor (JFET) is a voltage-controlled unipolar majority-carrier semiconductor device. Drain current in the saturation/pinch-off region follows Shockley's equation:
I_D = I_DSS × [ 1 - (V_GS / V_P) ]^2  (for V_DS ≥ V_GS - V_P)
The reverse-biased gate-source p-n junction results in exceptionally high input resistance (10^8 to 10^9 Ω).
❓ Essential Viva-Voce Questions
Q1: Define Pinch-Off Voltage (V_P) and Drain Saturation Current (I_DSS).
V_P is the drain-to-source voltage at which channel depletion regions touch and current saturates when V_GS = 0V. I_DSS is the maximum drain current when V_GS = 0V in the saturation region.
Q2: Why is a JFET referred to as a "unipolar" device?
Because conduction relies solely on majority carriers (electrons in N-channel JFET, holes in P-channel JFET), unlike BJTs where both electrons and holes participate.
Q3: What is Transconductance (g_m) of a JFET?
g_m = ΔI_D / ΔV_GS at constant V_DS. It measures how effectively the gate voltage controls the drain current, typically expressed in mA/V (or µS).
⚠️
Lab Pro-Tip: Never forward-bias the gate-source junction (keep V_GS ≤ 0V for N-channel JFET), as excessive gate current will destroy the junction.

CO4 ⚡ Falstad Interactive J, K logic inputs + Clock pulse trigger
Simulate ↗
💡 Core Theory & Characteristic Equation
The JK Flip-Flop resolves the undefined/forbidden state (S=R=1) of the SR flip-flop. When J=K=1, the output toggles on each clock pulse:
Q_(n+1) = J·Q'_n + K'·Q_n  |  J=1, K=1 ⇒ Q_(n+1) = Q'_n (Toggle)
States: J=0,K=0 (Hold) | J=0,K=1 (Reset=0) | J=1,K=0 (Set=1) | J=1,K=1 (Toggle).
❓ Essential Viva-Voce Questions
Q1: What is the "Race-Around Condition" in a JK flip-flop?
In a level-triggered JK flip-flop when J=K=1, if clock pulse duration t_p exceeds propagation delay Δt (t_p > Δt), the output toggles repeatedly and unpredictably during the single clock pulse, creating an indeterminate final state.
Q2: How is Race-Around eliminated in practice?
1) By using Edge-Triggering (narrow clock spike), or 2) By implementing a Master-Slave JK Flip-Flop architecture where Master latches on clock HIGH and Slave transfers to output on clock LOW.
Q3: What is the difference between synchronous inputs (J, K) and asynchronous inputs (PRESET, CLEAR)?
J and K inputs are sampled synchronously on the active clock edge. PRESET and CLEAR are asynchronous override inputs that set or clear the output immediately, regardless of clock signal.
⚠️
Lab Pro-Tip: In IC 7476, PRESET' and CLEAR' are active LOW. Make sure to connect both to +5V (logic 1) during standard clocked testing.

SSGMCE IT Lab Guidelines & Breadboard Best Practices

  • Always power down before wiring: Never insert or remove ICs from breadboards or trainers while DC power supply is ON.
  • Verify polarity: Double-check +5V and Ground leads before switching ON the bench power supply. A reversed supply damages TTL ICs instantly.
  • Decoupling capacitors: In hardware circuits, place a 0.1 µF ceramic capacitor across V_CC and GND near the IC to suppress high-frequency supply spikes.
  • Use standard wire colors: Red for +5V / +12V, Black for GND, Blue for -12V, Yellow/Green for signal inputs, White/Orange for outputs.
  • Measure carefully with CRO/DSO: Ensure the oscilloscope ground crocodile clip is connected to circuit ground only—never to an active node or signal terminal.
